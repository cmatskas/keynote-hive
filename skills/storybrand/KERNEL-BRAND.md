# AWS "Kernel" Brand: Voice & Verbal Identity Reference
### Agent Writing Guidance — Extracted from Sections 1 & 2

---

## PART 1: BRAND FOUNDATION (The "Why" Behind the Words)

### Core Belief
> **"We believe there is a builder in everyone, ready to turn their ambition into action."**

Every piece of writing should ultimately serve this belief. The reader is a builder (or a potential one). Your job as a writer is to move them from *ambition* → *action*.

### Brand Persona: "Every Maker's Champion"
- **AWS is NEVER the hero. The customer is the hero.** AWS is the supportive force *behind* the builder.
- **Primary enemy to overcome:** self-doubt. Writing should build confidence, not showcase AWS's greatness.
- **Litmus test:** If a sentence makes AWS look impressive, rewrite it to make the *reader* feel capable.

### Relationship to Amazon
- Amazon is a **"silent credential"**—it lends trust but is never the focus.
- Don't lead with the Amazon association; let it sit quietly in the background.

### The Five Personality Traits
Use these as tone checks. Good copy should feel like it embodies several at once.

| Trait | What It Sounds Like in Writing | Watch Out For |
|-------|-------------------------------|---------------|
| **Encouraging** | Affirms the reader's capability; forward-looking | Don't tip into cheerleading or empty hype |
| **Candid** | Direct, honest, no spin; admits hard things | Don't be blunt to the point of cold |
| **Curious** | Asks questions, explores, shows genuine interest | Don't be vague or aimless |
| **Ingenious** | Clever, resourceful, shows a smarter way | Don't be showy or overly clever for its own sake |
| **Determined** | Grounded, persistent, action-oriented | Don't be rigid or preachy |

## PART 2: VERBAL IDENTITY (The "How" of Writing)

### The Guiding Principle: "Meet & Move"
Every piece of communication does two things:
1. **Meet** people where they actually are—in their real context, challenges, and language.
2. **Move** them toward action—give them a next step, a reason, a spark.

**Agent rule of thumb:** Before finalizing any copy, ask: *"Does this meet the reader in their reality? Does it move them somewhere?"* If it does neither, cut it.

---

### The Five Voice Tenets

#### 1. Delight in Doing
- Celebrate the *act* of building, not just outcomes.
- Convey energy and momentum around the work itself.
- **Do:** "You start with a single function and watch it scale."
- **Avoid:** dry, purely transactional descriptions of features.

#### 2. You First
- Lead with the reader ("you"), not with AWS ("we"/"our").
- Frame everything around the reader's goals and reality.
- **Do:** "You ship faster when infrastructure gets out of your way."
- **Avoid:** "AWS provides infrastructure that enables faster shipping."

#### 3. Double Take
- Write something that makes the reader pause and reconsider.
- Use a fresh angle, an unexpected turn, a surprising truth.
- **Do:** "Most outages aren't loud. They're the slow ones you didn't see coming."
- **Avoid:** predictable, template-sounding statements.

#### 4. Perfect is Boring
- Embrace personality, texture, and a human voice over polished sterility.
- Real and slightly imperfect beats flawless and forgettable.
- **Do:** conversational rhythm, varied sentence length, a bit of edge.
- **Avoid:** corporate perfection that feels machine-written.

#### 5. Make it Feel Real
- Ground claims in concrete specifics and lived reality.
- Use real numbers, real scenarios, real proof.
- **Do:** "Cut deployment time from 40 minutes to under 3."
- **Avoid:** "dramatically faster deployments."

---

### Practical Writing Rules (Style Mechanics)

| Rule | Guidance |
|------|----------|
| **Tense** | Use **present tense.** It's active, immediate, and puts the reader in the moment. |
| **Proof** | Be **specific with real numbers.** Concrete evidence over adjectives. |
| **Hype** | **Skip hype and buzzwords.** No "revolutionary," "game-changing," "best-in-class." |
| **Jargon** | **Avoid jargon.** If a term needs decoding, replace or explain it. |
| **Altitude** | **Speak at eye level.** Never talk down; never inflate. Peer-to-peer, builder-to-builder. |

## QUICK-REFERENCE CHECKLIST (For the Agent)

Before finalizing any copy, verify:

- [ ] **Reader is the hero** — AWS is the supportive force, not the star
- [ ] **Builds confidence** — counters self-doubt rather than boasting
- [ ] **Meets & Moves** — grounded in reality AND points to action
- [ ] **Leads with "you"** — reader-first framing, not "we/our"
- [ ] **Present tense** — active and immediate
- [ ] **Specific proof** — real numbers, real scenarios, no vague claims
- [ ] **No hype, no jargon, no buzzwords**
- [ ] **Eye-level tone** — peer-to-peer, never condescending or inflated
- [ ] **Has personality** — "perfect is boring"; sounds human
- [ ] **Passes trait check** — reads as encouraging, candid, curious, ingenious, and/or determined

## MINI STYLE TRANSFORMATIONS (Before → After)

**Example A — Feature announcement**
- ❌ *Before:* "AWS is proud to launch a revolutionary new service that provides best-in-class scalability."
- ✅ *After:* "You can go from one user to a million without rewriting a line. It's live today."

**Example B — Benefit statement**
- ❌ *Before:* "Our solution enables organizations to optimize operational efficiency."
- ✅ *After:* "You spend less time firefighting and more time building. Teams here have cut incident response from hours to minutes."

**Example C — Getting started**
- ❌ *Before:* "AWS offers a comprehensive suite of tools designed to facilitate developer productivity."
- ✅ *After:* "Start with one line of code. See it running in your browser before your coffee's cold."
